A mirrored copy, and what happens when it lands on its source

nameCopy had only ever been exercised against translate, with the copy
placed 200 mm away. Two things about that fixture were doing quiet work,
and both are wrong for a Mirror feature.

It was a translation. A reflection is the transform most likely to break
anything leaning on geometry rather than provenance: a mirrored face has the
source's area and shape, so a tie-breaker comparing measurements sees a twin
where a translation gave it a stranger.

And the copy was far away. Bodies 200 mm apart fuse without a single face
splitting, so every existing copy test took the easy path through propagate.
The point of a mirror is that the copy lands against the original.

Three contact cases, chosen by mirror plane: clear at x = -100, face to face
at x = 0, overlapping at x = 30. All three come out with unnamed() and
collisions() empty. The overlap is the one worth having, 36000 mm3 against
the source's 24000, so the bodies really merged, into pieces whose twins
across the mirror plane have identical areas. That is the tie the
discriminator refuses to guess at, and it resolves it.

The overlap case was written to accept either outcome, clean names or an
honest NamingLost, because it was not obvious which it would reach. Under a
mutation that stops nameCopy stamping it passed by taking the refusal
branch, while five other cases failed. A test that accepts both cannot
notice the day it stops being the first one, so the hedge is gone and the
same mutation now fails six.
